Citizen development requests → national priority

Turning what citizens say into what governments build.

Development requests are collected by voice, text and messaging in any language, then fused with demographic, infrastructure and public-investment data to tell policymakers where money is most needed — and, critically, where nobody is looking.

The loudest voice is not the greatest need

Request volume measures who can complain. Demand here is divided by an expected-participation index built from literacy, smartphone access and urbanisation, so twenty requests from an excluded district outweigh twenty from a connected one.

Silence is a signal, not an absence

A district that sends nothing still surfaces on administrative data alone and is flagged silent — an outreach gap to close, never a reason to deprioritise.

Committed money lowers priority

Where a funded project already exists, priority is discounted — but never to zero, because a budget line is not delivered infrastructure. What remains are the genuine blind spots.

Every number is attributable

Each recommendation decomposes into weighted factors that sum exactly to its score, with a counterfactual and the citizen requests behind it. Weights are adjustable, because ranking is policy, not physics.

Prototype. Administrative names and populations are real; all indices and investment records are synthetic demonstration data and must not be cited.
